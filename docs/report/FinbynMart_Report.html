Finbyn Mart

Indian B2B Marketplace: Complete Project Report

How it works, which market problems it solves, what is built and what is not yet built
Prepared 12 November 2026 from the project repository (CLAUDE.md, DESIGN.md, PAGE_TRACKER.md and source code)

1. What is Finbyn Mart?

Finbyn Mart is an Indian B2B (business to business) marketplace website. Businesses that want to buy goods (buyers) can find suppliers, post what they need, ask for prices and compare offers. Businesses that sell (suppliers) can list products, receive buyer inquiries, send quotations and manage orders and stock.

The brand is teal and gold. The site is a single Next.js application with its own sign-in, a MongoDB database for accounts and business records, and separate areas (portals) for buyers and suppliers.

2. The market problem it addresses

Note: this section describes the general B2B sourcing problem the product is designed around. It is context, not measured market data. The repository contains no market research figures.
Problem for buyersHow Finbyn Mart is designed to help
Hard to find the right supplier for a productSearchable catalogue by category, product and supplier, with filters and a product comparison tool.
Asking many suppliers for prices is slow and scattered (calls, WhatsApp, e-mail)One structured inquiry or requirement form; replies and quotations arrive in the buyer's own portal.
Offers are hard to compare and change over timeQuotations have versions; buyers can accept, reject or request a revision, and compare versions.
No record of what was asked and agreedInquiries, quotations and orders are stored with status history.
Problem for suppliersHow Finbyn Mart is designed to help
Hard to reach serious buyersA public supplier profile and product listings visible to buyers.
Leads arrive in many places and are lostOne inquiry inbox per company with responses and clarifications kept in one conversation.
Quoting is manual and error-proneQuotation builder with exact money calculation done on the server, versions and expiry.
Stock and order tracking in notebooks or spreadsheetsInventory with an audit ledger, an orders list and basic analytics.

3. How it works (step by step)

Buyer journey

  • Visit the public site, browse categories or search products and suppliers.
  • Register as a buyer and sign in. The server checks the account's role and sends the buyer to the Buyer Portal.
  • Send an inquiry to a supplier, or post a requirement.
  • Receive responses and quotations; accept, reject or ask for a revision.
  • Track orders, save products and suppliers, compare products, manage addresses, notifications, security and support tickets.

Supplier journey

  • Register as a supplier. A company record is created for the account.
  • Sign in and land on the Supplier Dashboard. Sellers are linked to the company by the platform (a company cannot claim one itself).
  • Read buyer inquiries addressed to the company's sellers and reply.
  • Create, submit, revise or withdraw quotations (the buyer decides to accept or reject).
  • View orders, record stock, review analytics, edit the company profile and create product drafts.
Separate portals. Buyers never see supplier pages and suppliers never see buyer pages. Every portal page and API checks the signed-in user's role on the server, and a supplier can only read its own company's records.

6. Quality and safety rules the project follows

  • No fake data shown as real. Sample content is flagged. Forms that have no backend say so honestly; success messages appear only after the server confirms.
  • Money is exact. Amounts are whole minor units (paise) calculated on the server; currencies are never mixed or converted.
  • Versioned quotations. A sent version never changes; a revision is a new version.
  • Safe stock. Inventory changes only through an audit ledger; negative stock is refused; "not tracked" is never shown as zero.
  • Security basics. Server-side role checks, per-company data isolation, input validation, rate limiting on writes, same-origin checks, private (no-store) responses for personal data.
  • Accessibility and responsive design are required for every page (keyboard use, labels, contrast, mobile layouts).

7. What is finished and what is not

Working (built, not yet fully verified)

  • Public discovery site and the complete buyer portal.
  • Large part of the supplier portal as listed above.
  • Real accounts, inquiries, quotations, orders and inventory records in MongoDB.

Not built or blocked

GapDetail
Order creationNothing creates an order yet (no checkout and no quotation-to-order step), so orders, stock reservations and dispatch are empty or unsupported.
PaymentsNo payment system; payment analytics show "unavailable".
VerificationNo supplier verification or review process; sellers are never labelled verified.
Admin and moderationNo admin portal. Product publishing and approval workflow do not exist, so supplier product drafts cannot go live.
Notifications by e-mailNo e-mail or SMS delivery or scheduled jobs; in-app notices only.
Catalogue in databasePublic catalogue is still CSV, not MongoDB; supplier-created products are not yet shown publicly.
Supplier pages pendingSupplier inquiries list page, product editing of live products, company media and draft profile revisions.
Quality evidenceNo automated test framework or CI; most pages have not had full responsive, accessibility or load testing. Lighthouse results have not been measured. Deployment is unverified.
Known security itemsNo site-wide security headers yet; role enforcement is basic (a single owner role per company).
Important: the older audit in docs/FINBYN_COMPLETE_PROJECT_AUDIT.md is dated 2 November 2026 and is now out of date: it predates the buyer and supplier portals.

8. Recommended next steps

  1. Define and build the order-confirmation workflow (quotation accepted to order), then stock reservation and dispatch.
  2. Build the admin portal: supplier verification, product moderation, category control.
  3. Move the catalogue into MongoDB and connect published supplier products to the public site.
  4. Add e-mail delivery and a scheduler for notices, order and inquiry reminders.
  5. Add a payment integration or clearly separate offline payment records.
  6. Add automated tests, security headers, performance measurement and a verified deployment.

9. Summary

Finbyn Mart is a working foundation of an Indian B2B marketplace: buyers can discover suppliers and manage inquiries and quotations, and suppliers can respond, quote and track stock and activity, all with strict portal separation and honest handling of missing capabilities. To become a full marketplace it still needs order creation, payments, verification and admin tools, real catalogue data in the database, and proper testing and deployment.
